  A NOTE OF THANKS
  ----------------

  These games represent something remarkable. They were written by small
  teams — often single bedrooms, often teenagers — and yet they burned
  themselves into the memories of an entire generation. The rubber keys,
  the loading screens, the tension of a cassette that might just fail at
  98%... and then the reward of a world opening up on a 48K machine that
  by rights shouldn't have been able to hold it.

  The developers and artists behind these titles — many working at
  companies like Ultimate Play the Game, Ocean, Software Projects, Odin
  Computer Graphics, Incentive, Design Design, Hewson, and countless 
  others — built something that still holds up. The invention, the 
  humour, the sheer stubbornness of making isometric 3D work on a 
  Speccy... it deserves to be celebrated and kept alive.

  So here is Volume I. Load it up. Enjoy them all over again.

  And a warm, heartfelt thank you to the people who made this distribution
  possible:

    akeley       — for the curated top-100 collection that forms the heart
                   of this release.
                   https://archive.org/details/zxspectrum-top-100

    Bob Fossil   — for tireless documentation and passion for the platform.
                   http://www.thefossilrecord.co.uk/coding/100-machine-code/

    Miguel Guerreiro & Mario Prato
                 — creators of esxDOS, the firmware that makes DivMMC
                   such a joy to use.
                   https://www.esxdos.org/index.html

    Cesar Hernandez Bano
                 — creator of ZEsarUX, an emulator built with love and
                   extraordinary depth.
                   https://github.com/chernandezba/zesarux

    Steve Wetherill
                 — who ported Manic Miner and Jet Set Willy (game 130)
                   to the Amstrad CPC at Software Projects, expanding
                   JSW from 60 to 132 screens and composing its music.
                   He went on to Nodes of Yesod, Robin of the Wood, and
                   Projectyle at Odin, and kept going from there.
                   https://stevewetherill.substack.com/p/chapter-7-the-astronaut-and-the-sleeping

  Thank you all for keeping the Spectrum alive and powerful.
